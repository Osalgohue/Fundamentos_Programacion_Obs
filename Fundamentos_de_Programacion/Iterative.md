**Iterative** means involving the repetition of a process, sequence of steps, or cycle of actions—usually with the goal of improving, refining, or approaching a desired result step-by-step.
Instead of trying to get everything right on the first try, an iterative approach does a little bit, reviews the outcome, learns from it, and repeats the cycle (each cycle is called an _iteration_).

---

What It Means in Different Fields

- **Software & IT:** Writing code, running loops, or building an app in small, repeating cycles (like Agile or Scrum) so developers can test, find bugs, and release updates gradually.

- **Project Management & Design:** Creating a basic draft or prototype (Minimum Viable Product or MVP), gathering user feedback, and tweaking the design in the next round rather than planning a massive, rigid launch all at once.

- **Mathematics & Science:** Running a calculation or equation over and over, feeding each output back into the formula as the new input until the numbers converge on an accurate solution.

- **Education & Skill Learning:** Repeating a practice exercise, experiment, or project multiple times until you master the technique or achieve the right outcome ("practice makes perfect").

---

Key Subtopics & Related Concepts

- **Iterative vs. Incremental:** While often used together, _iterative_ means repeating and refining the same thing to make it better, whereas _incremental_ means building and delivering work in separate chunks or pieces.

- **Iteration (Noun):** A single pass or cycle through the repeating process.

- **Iteratively (Adverb):** Doing something in a repetitive, step-by-step manner.
